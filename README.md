# Life Happens

A playful race through life about how everyday money decisions compound over a lifetime.
Start at 23 with a random life (name, city, job, salary, maybe an education loan) and ride (by bus, Metro, scooter or car) down a road toward the finish line at 80: payday coins, birthday flags, a "?" block whenever life happens, a short pit stop every 5 years, and an original kart-style chiptune soundtrack. A whole life takes about 7 minutes, on one phone screen.

**The goal:** reach 80 rich *and* happy. One ❤️ Happiness number (0–100) is the score; four tiles show Savings, Invested, Loans and Debt at all times. Money is the fuel.

- **Play:** https://shubhamnov.github.io/Life-Happens/
- **Tests:** open `tests.html` from the same server (for example `python3 -m http.server`, then visit `http://localhost:8000/tests.html`). It loads `index.html?test=1&seed=N` in a hidden frame and runs the PRD scenario suite plus financial invariants. Same seed, same run.

Everything is a single static `index.html` (HTML, CSS, JS). No build step, backend, account or tracking. Fonts load from Google Fonts; everything else is inline.
Only the best lifetime happiness and sound/speed preferences are kept in the browser's local storage.

## Decisions on the PRD's open questions (§22)

These are game assumptions, documented in code and in the in-game About panel.

1. **Investment returns:** growth money follows a market index (its own seeded generator): a 13.5% trend, a swing that wanders within ±10% of it, and crashes. A crash comes about once in 20 years (two or three in a life), takes 33–50% off within 8–14 months, then wins back half to three quarters of the fall over 2–3½ years; the rest is a lasting dent. Net of crashes a lifetime grows 11–13.5% a year (about 12.5% typical), every 20-year stretch beats inflation, and a bad decade can be as low as 3–7%. Booms and dips move the swing inside its band. **Savings earn 2.5% a year**, credited monthly.
2. **Settlement order:** market move → income → living and fixed costs → vehicle and home EMIs → debt interest and minimum repayment → (retired only) sell investments to cover the gap → anything still unpaid becomes unsecured debt → monthly SIP if everything was paid.
3. **Inability to sustain expenses:** when savings run out, investments are sold automatically (working or retired) to pay bills, EMIs and debt repayments, and a one-time expense paid from savings spills into investments before any debt. A month is *strained* only if something still could not be paid after both were used. Six strained months **in a row** end the run; one fully paid month resets the count.
4. **Tax:** salary is before tax. Income tax follows India's new regime for FY 2025-26, simplified (₹75,000 standard deduction, slabs from 0% to 30%, no tax up to ₹12 lakh taxable with marginal relief, 4% cess), with slabs indexed to in-game inflation. Bonuses are after tax; investment gains are untaxed.
5. **Family:** life stages follow fixed windows. Marriage is asked at 23–24 (yes, not sure, no); weddings happen from 25 and never after 38. Kids (0, 1 or 2) are discussed 1–2 years after the wedding, the second arrives 2–4 years after the first, and no births happen after 40. A wedding costs ₹10 lakh (simple), ₹20 lakh (big) or ₹40 lakh (lavish). The spouse does not earn: their living costs are ₹9,000/month. A birth costs ₹1.5 lakh (private hospital) or ₹20,000 (government). Children cost ₹9,000 (first) and ₹7,000 (second) per month until 21, plus school fees until 18, chosen at 4 and changeable any time in More → Monthly plan (a cheaper school saves the fees at once; a pricier one charges admission again): government ₹1,500, budget private ₹10,000 (about ₹1.2 lakh a year), private ₹29,000 (about ₹3.5 lakh a year) or international ₹75,000 a month, plus admission. Parents need support from your mid-40s and pass away some 16–20 years later (you are 60–68), when the support and their health add-on end. All at age-23 prices, inflation-indexed.
6. **Insurance:** health cover (chosen at the start) pays 65% of eligible bills for everyone on the policy. It never renews by itself: at the first pit stop 3+ years after buying (then about every 5 years) you renew it, renew it as family cover, or let it lapse, and the premium is re-set for your age and who is on it. It covers only you until you renew it as family cover; after a wedding or a child you are asked once. Parents are an optional add-on. More → Monthly power-ups lists what you have. Term cover (₹1 crore in today's money, until 65) has a premium locked at purchase, so buying young is cheapest; its disability benefit pays 20% of the cover if an accident stops you working for a year. The player never dies.
7. **Retirement:** no pension. From 40 (prompted at 60), salary stops; as at any age, investments are sold automatically when savings run short. This is disclosed when retiring.
8. **Event history:** kept per run for the end-of-run summary and list of major decisions.
9. **Save/resume:** not added; restart always starts a clean run.
10. **Tests:** seeded random generator (`?seed=`), a test API in `?test=1` mode, and `tests.html`.
11. **Happiness:** three hidden parts (time for yourself, family and friends, community life) each move 4% a month toward a target set by how you live: food, extras and social life, home and ride, savings cushion and debt, marriage, kids, pets, parents, work pace and life stage. Event choices add bumps that fade. Bumps you can buy again (a day with your people, volunteering, donating, trips) are a glow that fades in a few months (20% a month), so repeating them every year adds a few points, not a win. A single, frugal life heads to about 40; married with kids, friends and holidays to about 70. Choices never show their happiness effect up front (that steered players toward the ❤️ answer); the effect (❤️+ to ❤️+++ or 💔− to 💔−−−) appears only after you pick. The mix is 60% the weakest part + 40% the average, stretched onto 0–100 (`happyScale()`: (mix − 15) × 2), so a typical start shows about 50 and a well-balanced life 75+. The HUD flags the weakest part, and tapping ❤️ shows all three parts, where each is heading and what moves the weakest one. Lifetime happiness is the average over every month to 80 (months not reached count as zero). The ending combines it with net worth in today's money (₹1 crore+ is rich): Rich and Happy (65+), Happy Heart (65+), Rich but Restless, A Tough Road, Comfortably Off, Steady Cruiser, or Lived Fast Ran Out of Fuel if the money runs out.
12. **Big buys trade happiness:** a society flat (₹60L at age-23 prices) is great for community life but not showy; an independent villa (₹1.08Cr) is comfy and has the most show-off value but is lonelier; a flat near family (₹51L) is cheapest and close to family. Cars: hatchback (₹6L), 7-seater (₹10L, good for family) or big SUV (₹15L, most show-off value). Show-off value is shown but adds no happiness.
13. **Work pace comes from choices, not a dial:** a stretch project (2 years), weekend gigs (6 months), a startup (2 years) or weekend classes (1 year) mean Hustle: +1% raises, 1.5× promotion odds, less happiness. Slowing down after burnout means 2 years of Chill: −1%, 0.6×, more happiness. Then work returns to Balanced. On Hustle with family time running low, the game offers to slow down (at most every 5 years). Very low time for yourself for 9 months brings burnout (at most every 3 years).
14. **Fewer interruptions, faster life:** 0.45 s per month at 1× (speeds 1×, 2×, 4×), so a life takes about 7 minutes. Only big life moments stop the race. About 30 small surprises and ads per life slide in as cards below the race; none comes more than twice, and a repeat waits at least 15 years. With pit stops and life moments, a life has about 58 stops. There is no timer: the race stops until you tap a choice, so you play at your own pace. Choices are kept to a title and one short line.
15. **Pit stops:** every 5 years (ages 28, 33 … 78) a short lap report: happiness and net worth changes, one line about the lap, one tip and, when money is left over, a one-tap SIP start or top-up. The race waits until you tap Continue.
16. **Story card instead of a setup form:** pick 👨 Male or 👩 Female (remembered for the next life); each life starts with a random name, city, job, salary (₹35K–₹60K) and sometimes an education loan, renting and taking the bus or Metro. 🎲 New life rolls another. Start ▶ opens a 6-step animated setup (home, food, weekends, holidays, extras, health cover) showing each choice's monthly cost, its effect on happiness and what is left of the salary; Skip keeps the card as it is. Without cover, health cover is offered as a card in month 2 (default: take it).
17. **Idle cash:** savings earn 2.5% while prices rise 6% a year. About 6 months of expenses is a healthy cushion (`idleCash()`); anything above it shows as 💤 idle on the Savings tile, and the pit crew points it out.
18. **Ad breaks:** about once every 5½–8½ years (about 7 per life) an ad card slides onto the track, on its own seeded generator so ads never change the rest of a seeded life. Genuine (invest the idle cash or start a SIP), traps (“guaranteed” returns: most of the money is lost) and big buys on easy EMIs (big SUV, villa, flagship phone, a “must-have” for your wealth class, all through the normal bank check). Ads are meant to tempt and mislead: they quote your own savings (“₹12L in savings and still no car?”) and your wealth class (“Every upper middle class family drives an SUV”). Choices state the real cost in one line; the truth comes in the feedback. Not tapping scrolls past.
19. **Your wealth could have been:** the end screen shows up to three what-ifs, biggest first, each one change on its own with the same monthly market returns as your life, in today's money: idle cash above the cushion invested, the SIP started with the first salary (or 10% of pay if there was never a SIP), investments never sold to pay bills, no card-debt interest, no ad losses. The headline adds the biggest one to your real net worth. Each comes with a one-line take-home lesson. If you fell for an ad or a friend's tip, “Never invest or buy just because someone told you to” joins the take-home list; a run that ends broke opens with “Every failure is a lesson”.
20. **Wealth class and lessons:** no tags or recommendations on choices; you decide. `wealthClass()` names what advertisers think you are from net worth in today's money (🌱 Just starting, 🏠 Middle class from ₹5L, 🚗 Upper middle class from ₹25L, 💼 Affluent from ₹1Cr, 💎 Rich from ₹5Cr). It shows on every ad card, and the `adStatus` ad sells a “must-have” for that class (earbuds and a smartwatch, a big TV, a Europe holiday, a Swiss watch, a superbike). A mistake (a scheme, a hot tip that crashes, a phishing link, an impulse buy you could not really afford) shows as 📘 Lesson learnt. The first time a SIP is offered, a one-time “What's a SIP?” explainer pauses the race.
21. **Money check:** if expenses are above take-home pay for 2 months in a row, the race stops: trim (one step down on food, weekends and holidays, no extras), cut to basics, move the kids one step down to a cheaper school (when they are in a fee-paying one), choose in More → Monthly plan, or keep going. At most once a year.
22. **Job loss and lifestyle:** after a layoff (or an accident or illness that stops pay) you choose how to live with no salary: cut to basics, trim, or keep going, each showing how many months your savings and investments last. Back at work, a card asks whether to return to the old lifestyle (default: stay lean). A starter flat is not allowed with two kids.
23. **Two big shocks before 60:** every life has two scheduled at random (the first at 29–40, the second 8+ years later, by 57) on their own seeded generator. If a big shock (layoff, accident, divorce, serious illness, flood, parent's hospital bill) has not already happened by then, one is drawn from layoff, accident, serious illness (₹5–15L, 65% covered, 3 months off), flood (₹2–5L, never covered) or a parent's hospital bill. In simulated lives (20 each): taking every default and retiring at 60 runs out of money in about 3 in 10, mostly after 70; a frugal life never does; a big wedding plus two kids in private school goes broke in about 1 in 3, mostly in the 40s.
24. **Lifestyle creep and surprises:** when take-home pay reaches 1.8× what it was when you last set your lifestyle, friends expect an upgrade (default: keep living as you do; at most twice a life, 6+ years apart). Good surprises (windfalls, an employee-of-the-year award with Career XP) and tough calls (a big gig on the same weekend as your child's annual day, your anniversary trip or a friend's wedding) are cards.
25. **Insurance ads and lump sums:** insurance ads offer what you lack (health, term or parents' cover) or a misleading 3-in-1 plan: 10 years of premiums, about 4.5% a year, paid out after 15, 30% back if surrendered early (in Monthly plan). More → One-time → Invest a lump sum invests idle cash above 6 months, a quarter, half or all of your savings.
26. **Pit stops and retirement:** the pit stop shows take-home pay and monthly expenses (with ⚠️ when short). From 40 it adds a 🏖️ Retirement check (`retireOutlook()`): in today's money, investments grow about 6% a year after inflation, the SIP and leftover keep going in until 60, then living costs without kids, school, loans or term cover come out, and it says whether the money lasts past 80 or runs out at about what age. Once retired, if savings and investments cover under two years of costs, the race stops once: sell the home and rent a small flat, move in with your children (they cover home, food, outings and travel; your home is sold), cut to basics, or keep going. Running out after 65 ends as 🧓 Outlived the Money. The money check after 2 months over income is a red ⚠️ warning. Retiring opens a retirement plan: rent (while renting), food, weekends, holidays and extras, showing monthly costs, savings plus investments and how many years they last at those costs.
27. **Where to play:** players thought the race up top was the game. The first race opens with a one-time “How to play” card (also in More) showing the screen: the race is just to watch, money tiles below it, moves pop up under them, ☰ More for the plan. A strip on the race reads “Just watch the race · you play below 👇” for the first two years and “✋ Race stopped · your move is below 👇” while a card waits. Tapping the race no longer pauses; it points you below and highlights the card area and More. The race was made about a third shorter (about 140–175 px tall) so the money and your move get the screen.
28. **Travel is part of life:** players said there was no way to travel. Holidays are now a Monthly Plan line next to food and weekends (stay home, weekend getaways ₹3K, a big India trip a year ₹6K, abroad every year ₹15K a month), asked in profile setup and in the retirement plan. It is priced per traveller (a spouse counts fully, each child 60%, so a couple pays 2× and a family of four about 3.2×; holidays with family also fill Family & Friends, and each yearly family trip gives it an extra lift), so family holidays cost more. The fund pays for a trip once a year: a postcard on the race and a journal line, with a small happiness bump. More → Power-ups → Plan a dream trip books a one-off trip from savings only, once a year (hills, India, abroad or a bucket-list trip). Cutting back steps holidays down too. Home prices were raised to metro levels: a society flat is ₹60L at age-23 prices.

Other model rules: 6% inflation applied at the start of each simulated year (costs are stored at age-23 prices and priced with that year's factor, so nothing is inflated twice); a month-7 review raise, then a yearly appraisal of 11% to 28, 8.5% to 35, 6.5% to 45 and 4.5% after; promotions (at most every 2 years) are bigger and more likely early on (+27% before 28, +20% to 35, +12% to 45, then +6.5%; odds 12% + 6% per Career XP, scaled down with age); an education loan in some starting lives (₹8 lakh at 10% over 8 years, ≈₹12,100 EMI) raises the first salary by 25%; your own vehicle adds fuel (₹2,500/month two-wheeler, ₹8,000 car) on top of upkeep; a monthly SIP is invested after bills and skipped, without counting as strain, when cash is short; home loans at 8.5% over 20 years with 20% down + 7% fees, and a bank check that all EMIs stay within 50% of pay; events arrive every 20–32 months, with random openers so no two lives start alike; unsecured debt at 1%/month with a minimum repayment of max(3%, ₹2,000 indexed); vehicle loans at 12%/year over 36 months (two-wheeler ₹1L ≈ ₹3,321 EMI, car ₹6L ≈ ₹19,929 EMI); new loans need all EMIs, including the education loan, within 50% of take-home pay; a missed EMI becomes unsecured debt while the loan keeps amortising; selling a financed vehicle repays 80% of the remaining loan and the rest becomes debt; layoff severance = 1 month's notice pay + 15 days' pay per completed year in the job (capped at 12 months), then a job search of 6–18 months (random, up to 4 months longer after 45) without salary, or a 25% lower-paid role at once.

All values are illustrative. This is educational entertainment, not financial advice.
29. **Safe or growth (the second money decision):** investments have a safe part (7% a year, never falls) and a growth part (the market). The mix (More → Mix: 0, 25, 50 or 75% safe) moves existing money to match at today's prices and steers new SIP and lump-sum money. Forced sales take safe money first. Simulations (60 seeded lives per mix) showed safe money never ends a life richer, because 12.5% against 7% outruns even a crash at retirement. So the trade-off is stated honestly as wealth against calm: during a crash the Me ring drops in step with the share of money in growth (up to −12 working, −24 retired), and a retiree with five years of bills in savings plus safe money gets +6 Me. Result: comfortable lives end happier with a safe bucket at retirement (about half of them, +0.3 to +0.5 lifetime happiness, about 30% less money left at 80); tight lives go broke more often with one (2–4 more in 60), because they need the growth. A crash card appears three months into each crash (stay, move half to safe, sell a quarter); moving to safe mid-crash is flagged as locking in the loss. The pit crew suggests a safe bucket from 55 only when the pot covers 22+ years of costs. The retirement check blends the two returns by the current mix.

## Tools

Serve the repo (`python3 -m http.server 8765`), then:

- `node tools/run-tests.js` runs `tests.html` headless and exits non-zero on any failure.
- `node tools/simulate.js 40 out.json base,ret50` plays 40 seeded lives per style (styles are listed in the file).
- `node tools/summarize.js out.json` prints broke counts, median and 10th-percentile real net worth (₹ lakh) and happiness per style.
- `node tools/compare.js out.json base ret50` compares two styles on the same seeds.

Playwright must be installed (`npm i playwright`), or set `PW_CHROMIUM` to a Chromium binary.
30. **Health cover worth having:** it used to cost far more than it paid, because health bills stopped at 60. Now a hospital stay comes every 3–5 years from 60–63 (₹1–4L in today's money, 5% bigger per year past 60, for you or your spouse; the spouse is covered only on family cover). Health scares can come three times a life (8+ years apart) and a parent's hospital bill three times (6+ years apart, more likely once parents need support). The parents' add-on is ₹3,000 a month (was ₹5,000). No new cover past 65, from ads or power-ups, and the pit stop says so before you let it lapse near that age. From 55 cover is +6 Me ("as health worries grow") and no cover −5. Every claim now counts in the end summary. Over 150 seeded lives per style, cover leaves median wealth about equal, raises the 10th percentile from ₹34L to ₹74L (base family), keeps 2–4 more lives in 150 from going broke, and adds +0.5 lifetime happiness (+1.25 on a tight budget).
31. **Big buys worth something:** cars were 100% financed over 3 years at 12% (a hatchback cost about ₹20K a month before fuel) for +4 happiness, and the villa was out of reach and gave less happiness than renting. Now cars are 5-year loans at 9.5% (hatchback EMI about ₹12,600), with realistic fuel (₹5,000 / ₹7,000 / ₹9,500) and upkeep, and rings hatchback [6,4,2], 7-seater [5,10,2], SUV [10,4,-1]. The villa is ₹90 lakh (1.5× a society flat), ₹5,000 maintenance, rings [18,8,0], and its land grows 6.5% a year (flats 5%). Cutting to basics also sells a paid-off car or two-wheeler. Happiness weighs the weakest ring at 60% and Community is usually the weakest, which is why the flat near family and the society flat stay the happiest buys. Over 100 seeded lives per style with a bot that only buys when 15% of take-home stays free: flat near family +7.3 lifetime happiness, society flat +5.0, 7-seater +0.5, hatchback +0.65 (for about ₹1 crore less at 80 and 3 more lives broke in 100), villa about +1.4 for the 14 who could afford it, SUV −0.9 (the show-off trap).
